## 1. Why the expensive synths sound expensive

### 1.1 The common denominators

Across u-he, Ableton, Arturia, Xfer and the hardware makers, the "expensive" sound comes down to a short list of things, none of them magic:

1. Filters solved as implicit (zero-delay-feedback) nonlinear systems rather than unit-delay chains, so resonance stays correct under fast modulation and at high cutoffs.
2. Oversampling and/or antiderivative anti-aliasing (ADAA) around every nonlinearity, so distortion products are harmonic rather than aliased.
3. Per-voice component tolerance and slow drift (seeded per instance), so chords beat and detune the way a voice-card polysynth does.
4. Stage-by-stage saturation that is asymmetric and placed where the circuit puts it (mixer into filter, filter feedback path, output VCA).
5. Oscillators that are correctly band-limited (BLEP or mip-mapped tables) and, for stacks, use a perceptually tuned detune law rather than linear spread.

### 1.2 u-he Diva and Repro: component-level modelling, ZDF, quality modes

u-he's own page says Diva's filters "combine real-time circuit simulation with ZDF design", that ZDF "greatly reduces the delays that normally come with modelling analogue circuits, which yields more authentic resonance", and that Diva "is the first native software synth that applies methods from industrial circuit simulators in realtime". It lists oscillator models Triple VCO / Dual VCO / DCO / Dual VCO Eco / Digital and filter models Ladder (24 or 12 dB), Cascade, Multimode, Bite (Sallen-Key) and Uhbie (2-pole SVF), and a Trimmers panel with per-oscillator detune, voice variance and drift "to mimic component tolerances in real hardware" (https://u-he.com/products/diva/). The page admits the sound "comes at the cost of quite a high CPU-hit".

What the quality modes actually change: in the Xils ZDF thread, Urs Heckmann says Diva's Draft mode uses a unit-delay (z^-1) solver while Fast uses an iterative zero-delay solver at 12-bit accuracy, that Fast handles fast modulation much better, and that in Draft the resonance volume "goes completely out of whack" (https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=4921167). A Synthtopia commenter reports the same hierarchy from listening: some patches sound noticeably different in Draft because the filter responds differently, while Fast vs Divine is "much more subtle" (https://www.synthtopia.com/?p=37235). MusicRadar's review: in Divine mode a quad-core i7 MacBook Pro got crackles beyond two or three notes on some patches, but Diva was A/B'd against a room of vintage synths including a restored Minimoog Model D and "held its own"; verdict "sounds phenomenal", CPU hit "very brutal" (https://musicradar.com/reviews/tech/u-he-diva-542059).

The counter-view, which matters for the CPU budget: in the same Xils thread, mystran argues ZDF does not solve aliasing, that unit-delay filters "work fine apart from aliasing", and that the main benefit shows up under modulation; kv331 argues that with proper fudge-factoring plus at least 4x oversampling ZDF may be unnecessary; Urs notes real analogue filters often lose resonance at the extremes because of capacitors in the resonance path, and that Sallen-Key designs cannot hold constant Q (https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=4921167). Takeaway: ZDF is for correctness under modulation and at high cutoff; oversampling is for aliasing; you want both, but ZDF is cheap and oversampling is not.

Repro-1/Repro-5 are u-he's component-level Pro-One / Prophet-5 recreations (https://u-he.com/products/repro/); MusicRadar's Repro-1 review is at https://www.musicradar.com/reviews/u-he-repro-1.

### 1.3 Filter topologies: what each one does to the sound

The reference text is Vadim Zavalishin's "The Art of VA Filter Design" (NI PDF, v2.1.0: https://www.native-instruments.com/fileadmin/ni_media/downloads/pdf/VAFilterDesign_2.1.0.pdf; overview: https://www.dsprelated.com/showabstract/3910.php). It covers trapezoidal (TPT) integrators, zero-delay feedback solving, ladder, SVF, diode ladder, Sallen-Key and nonlinear variants. Useful open implementations of the same designs are Csound's `zdf_ladder` and `diode_ladder` opcodes (https://csound.com/manual/zdf_ladder.html, https://csound.com/manual/diode_ladder.html). The classic analysis of the Moog ladder for digital implementation is Stilson and Smith (https://levien.com/ladder.pdf; also https://www.musicdsp.org/en/latest/Filters/24-moog-vcf.html).

What is audibly different between the families (standard VA knowledge rather than one source):

- Transistor ladder (Moog): 4 cascaded one-pole stages with tanh nonlinearity per stage, global feedback; resonance steals bass ("bass loss" with high Q), self-oscillation is a clean sine, saturation in the feedback path softens resonance when driven. u-he calls this "Ladder"; Ableton Drift's Type I (DFM1) is in this family.
- OTA / CEM cascade (Jupiter, Prophet, SEM-adjacent): similar cascade but with OTA-style clipping; Diva calls it "Cascade".
- SEM / 2-pole SVF (Oberheim): 12 dB/oct, continuously variable LP/notch/HP, less bass loss, smoother resonance. Diva's "Uhbie". A hardware SEM measured in the KVR Minimoog shootout thread capped around 16.5 kHz (https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=6774316), i.e. real analogue filters are darker than textbook.
- Sallen-Key (MS-20, Korg 35): resonance is implemented with a feedback nonlinearity that clips hard, so it "screams" and cannot hold constant Q (Urs, Xils thread above). Diva's "Bite"; Ableton's Drift Type II is a Cytomic MS-20 model (https://cdm.link/2023/05/inside-ableton-drift/).

Ableton's Drift article is the most practical public account of how a 2023-era mainstream synth team chose filters: Type II is a "24 dB Cytomic MS-20-style filter, also used in other Ableton instruments"; Type I is Tony Hardie-Bick's DFM1 (from SuperCollider), updated by its author for vectorised rendering of four voices at once; they are labelled I and II rather than by slope "because they have different calibration and respond to frequencies differently"; mixer gain above 0 dB overdrives the filter input, near 6 dB "a stronger second stage engages", pre-filter clipping is asymmetrical "similar to a Moog Grandmother", and post-filter distortion affects resonance (https://cdm.link/2023/05/inside-ableton-drift/).

### 1.4 Oversampling and ADAA

- The ADAA family: Parker, Zavalishin and Le Bivic, DAFx 2016 (https://dafx.de/paper-archive/details/vem_XXF5qBbfiWOH2RVVAA), developed further by Bilbao, Esqueda, Parker and Valimaki; Holters (DAFx 2019) extends it to stateful systems. Jatin Chowdhury's notebook explains first and second order, the 0/0 ill-conditioning fallback (evaluate the nonlinearity at the midpoint when consecutive inputs are nearly equal), and notes first-order ADAA adds about half a sample of group delay, which detunes resonant feedback loops unless you shorten the delay line (https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jatin/Notebooks/adaa.html).
- Numbers: Vicanek's paper gives the rule of thumb that for a hard-driven saturator with roughly 1/f falloff, 2x oversampling buys about 6 dB and 4x about 12 dB of alias reduction on its own; ADAA alone is strong on low-frequency aliases and weaker at high frequencies; ADAA combined with 4x oversampling makes aliasing "practically a non-issue even for a hard clipper"; his recommended shaper is f(x)=x/sqrt(1+x^2), whose antiderivative is sqrt(1+x^2), which "performs about as well as tanh" for harmonic content and is cheaper; the transparent variant needs two compensating one-pole filters for about 4 dB of HF loss at Nyquist (https://www.vicanek.de/articles/AADistortion.pdf). There is a 2025 DAFx paper on the topic too (https://dafx25.dii.univpm.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/DAFx25_paper_30.pdf).
- Oscillators: PolyBLEP corrects about two samples per discontinuity with a trivial polynomial, leaves some audible aliasing as the fundamental nears Nyquist at 44.1/48 kHz, and slightly dulls the top; 2x oversampled PolyBLEP or DPW measure better; recommendations include crossfading to a Fourier series or sine near Nyquist (https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=5269992; see also the synth-diy BLEP thread https://synth-diy.org/pipermail/synth-diy/2021-February/167031.html).
- The engine already runs 2x with half-band decimation. The evidence says: keep 2x as the global rate, and add ADAA inside every waveshaper and filter nonlinearity rather than going to 4x globally (see section 5 for budget).

### 1.5 Drift, component tolerance, and the "serial number" trick

Drift's defining feature is its namesake parameter: "random detuning to both oscillators and the filter frequency. Each voice gets its own randomization, which mimics the component variation between voice cards in analog synths"; higher drift "widens the oscillator and filter detuning, producing slow beating"; crucially, "each new instance is seeded from a serial number, which is saved with presets so the same sound recalls exactly" (https://cdm.link/2023/05/inside-ableton-drift/). Diva's Trimmers do the same with per-oscillator detune, voice variance and drift (https://u-he.com/products/diva/). Werner et al measured the actual tolerance sources in the 808: plus or minus 20% capacitors and plus or minus 5% resistors, affecting mainly gain, centre frequency, Q and decay (https://dafx14.fau.de/papers/dafx14_kurt_james_werner_a_physically_informed,_ci.pdf). A KVR poster complains that Arturia's Matrix-12 emulation's oscillators are "too stable" (https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=6774316), which is the sound people are describing when they call a synth "cheap" or "static".

### 1.6 Unison and the JP-8000 supersaw

Adam Szabo's thesis reverse-engineered the JP-8000: detune is non-linear across the six side oscillators, fitted as a degree-11 polynomial of the detune knob; the six side-oscillator offsets are multiples -0.11002313, -0.06288439, -0.01952356, +0.01991221, +0.06216538, +0.10745242 of (freq x curve(detune)); centre gain is linear (-0.55366x + 0.99785) and side gain quadratic (-0.73764x^2 + 1.2841x + 0.044372) in the mix knob; the sum is high-passed at the fundamental; oscillator phases are randomised at note-on (SuperCollider port with the coefficients: https://gist.github.com/audionerd/fe50790b7601cba65ddd855caffb05ad; context: https://schollz.com/til/221103; KVR discussion: https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=8572590). Schollz quotes the reasoning: the non-linear curve "increases the sweet spots". Elektron's Digitone II ships this idea as the "Swarmer" machine, "a supersaw-style stacked oscillator, not limited to saw waves", with harmonic width, detune, animation and noise modulation (https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/elektron-digitone-ii).

### 1.7 Wavetable: interpolation and mip-mapping

Serum 2 keeps wavetable as its core engine and adds Multisample (SFZ import), Sample, Granular (up to 256 grains) and Spectral (resynthesis with independent time, pitch and "timbre shifting") oscillators, "true FM" warp, dual warp, dual filters in series or parallel, up to 10 LFOs with rates to 1000 Hz and Lorenz/Rossler chaos modes, and FX including a Bode frequency shifter, convolution, and three reverb types (https://static.xferrecords.com/Serum%202%20What's%20New.pdf). MusicTech scores it 8/10, calls the spectral oscillator "mind-blowing", multisample "a little gimmicky", and says it is "more CPU-hungry than ever" (https://musictech.com/reviews/software-instruments/xfer-records-serum-2-review). Vital (free, GPL) introduced "spectral warping" of wavetables (https://musictech.com/news/matt-tytel-vital-free-synth/). Standard practice for alias-free wavetables (not from a single fetched source; the KVR mip-mapping thread failed to load) is one band-limited table per octave or per half-octave generated by FFT truncation, crossfaded between levels, with cubic or Hermite read; the sampler already has Hermite, so the oscillator can share it.

### 1.8 FM, phase distortion, physical modelling, granular

- FM feedback: OPL/DX-style feedback adds the operator's output back into its own phase (max around plus or minus 4 pi on OPL chips), and the chips average the two previous output samples to tame the chaos; the DX7 uses a one-sample delay buffer; without the averaging a self-fed operator goes from saw-like to noise (https://community.vcvrack.com/t/frequency-modulation-feedback-behaviour/9981). Through-zero FM (TZFM) is the analogue version that lets the modulated frequency go negative (https://www.modulargrid.net/e/timo-rozendal-fm-ogre). Digitone II's FM Tone engine is 4-op, 8 algorithms, with feedback and ratio snapping; its FM Drum engine is "a 3-operator FM body feeding a wavefolder" plus a transient section mixing noise and PCM attacks, each with independent hold-decay envelopes (https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/elektron-digitone-ii).
- Phase distortion (Casio CZ): one sine table, the phase counter is warped by one of eight transfer shapes, the DCW envelope sets the amount and sounds like a filter sweep with no filter; three "resonant" shapes run a sine at a DCW-controlled multiple of the fundamental, windowed by a ramp/trapezoid/triangle that resets each cycle (a soft hard-sync), which avoids discontinuities; MusicRadar warns PD "can easily generate aliasing when you push it hard" (https://musicradar.com/news/tech/what-is-phase-distortion-synthesis-618081). Digitone II added phase distortion in its Wavetone machine (https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/elektron-digitone-ii).
- Physical modelling: Karplus-Strong (delay line + two-point average loop filter H(z)=0.5+0.5z^-1, noise burst excitation) and Jaffe/Smith's Extended KS add tuning, brightness and dynamics control and lead to the digital waveguide view (https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/Karplus_Strong_Algorithms.html; book root https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp). Ableton Collision is the modal reference: beater exciter (stiffness, noise) plus a filtered noise exciter, two resonators in serial or parallel of type Beam, Marimba, String, Membrane, Plate, Pipe, Tube, with decay, brightness, inharmonics, hit position, listening position, material, ratio/opening/radius, harmonic vs disharmonic overtones, and MPE since Live 11.3 (https://www.ableton.com/en/packs/collision/). Pigments 6's headline addition was exactly this: a Modal engine with Collision and Friction exciters and beam/string resonators, plus a vocoder and new filters (https://www.musicradar.com/music-tech/synths/arturia-pigments-review). Digitone II's Comb+/Comb- filters with full key tracking "can produce Karplus-Strong-style physical modelling sounds", and the reviewer calls the comb-filter piano "surprisingly convincing" (https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/elektron-digitone-ii).
- Granular: Robert Henke's Granulator III is the clearest public design: grain size 2 ms to 2 s; Classic mode (two overlapping grains per channel with an envelope that morphs from sharp attack/exponential decay to smooth crossfade), Loop mode, and Cloud mode (up to 20 unsynced grains per channel per note, fixed window "for CPU reasons"); Variation randomises position, size, amplitude and pitch per grain; Spread detunes and offsets left/right grain start times; grain-size changes wait for the current grain to finish to avoid clicks; 8 voices fixed; two SVFs in series; for in-tune playback he suggests a grain size of 3.822 ms with full key tracking (https://roberthenke.com/technology/granulator3.html; SOS: https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/ableton-live-12-granulator-iii).

### 1.9 What reviewers and users say they hear

- Diva: "held its own" against a restored Minimoog; the filters are singled out as the reason (MusicRadar, above). Users: Draft vs Fast audibly different in filter response, Fast vs Divine subtle (Synthtopia comments, above).
- Drift: Ableton built it to be a "best friend synth" with "life on its own", versus their previous synths which the designer calls "construction kits" (https://cdm.link/2023/05/inside-ableton-drift/).
- Moog Model D app: "excellent, wholly authentic", "among the richest and most authentic" emulations on any platform; filter "highly squelchy" (https://musicradar.com/reviews/moog-model-d-for-ios).
- Pigments 6: "the best softsynth on the market right now", "increasingly little in that vintage synth collection that couldn't be convincingly recreated" (MusicRadar, above).
- Roland TR-8S (ACB): "high-quality, largely convincing recreations", but "the master distortion and drive lack warmth, which the reviewer links to the unit's digital design" (https://www.musicradar.com/reviews/roland-tr-8s-rhythm-performer). That last point is the most useful warning in this whole report: a clean engine with a cheap drive stage reads as digital no matter how good the oscillators are.
- Analog Rytm: kicks and snares "very good", the hybrid sound "really shines" once it passes through the analogue filter, distortion and compressor (https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/elektron-analog-rytm).
- Digitakt II: "it also just sounds immense", credited to the send effects, drive and compressor (https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/elektron-digitakt-ii).

## 2. Drums

### 2.1 Kick: what the 808 circuit actually does

Werner, Abel and Smith's DAFx-14 model is the best public description (https://dafx14.fau.de/papers/dafx14_kurt_james_werner_a_physically_informed,_ci.pdf; also https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/details/hwkd1Lfe8vmtOxCEjsNY-g):

- A 1 ms trigger pulse, amplitude 4 to 14 V set by accent, through a pulse shaper (passive low shelf + diode; falling edge clipped to about one diode drop below ground).
- A bridged-T resonator in the op-amp feedback path rings as a decaying pseudo-sinusoid; model centre frequency about 49.5 Hz.
- Decay is a high-shelf in the feedback path: less attenuation = longer ring.
- Attack pitch shift: for roughly 6 ms a transistor lowers the bridged-T resistance, raising Q and centre frequency; "too short to hear as a pitch change, but it makes the attack punchier".
- A retriggering pulse (high-pass + diode clipper) re-excites the resonator after the attack so there is no amplitude jump when the frequency drops back.
- Leakage through the retrigger circuit "accounts for 'sighing' of the bass drum's pitch" over the multi-second tail.
- The paper's key claim: circuit architecture and sub-circuit interactions matter more than subtle device nonlinearities. Unit-to-unit variation comes from plus or minus 20% capacitors and plus or minus 5% resistors.

Design implication for the kick: two pitch envelopes (a ~6 ms attack "Q kick" and a slow multi-second "sigh"), a clicky transient from the shaped pulse, a decay implemented as feedback-path high-shelf damping rather than a plain amplitude envelope, and no "machine-gun" artefact on fast retrigger.

### 2.2 Hats and cymbals: the 808 partial set

The companion ICMC/SMC-2014 paper on the cymbal/hi-hat (https://speech.di.uoa.gr/ICMC-SMC-2014/images/VOL_2/1453.pdf): six Schmitt-trigger square-wave oscillators shared by hat, cymbal and cowbell, nominal frequencies 205.3, 369.6, 304.4, 522.7 Hz, plus two trimmed oscillators nominally 800 and 540 Hz (ranges 359.4 to 1149.9 and 254.3 to 627.2 Hz), about 48% duty; summed passively; two band-passes centred near 3440 Hz and 7100 Hz emphasise upper overtones and kill the fundamentals; three Sallen-Key high-passes after the "swing" VCAs, the op-amp ones producing a resonant peak near 10.5 kHz; the swing VCAs are nonlinear common-emitter stages whose clipping is intended. The paper explicitly says there is no noise generator in the 808 hat and that the "multiple noise sources" claim in a Novation manual is false: the metallic sound is filtered square-wave clusters with inharmonic, tightly packed partials. Envelopes: 1 ms trigger smoothed by a one-pole, then fast attack / exponential release with time constant = decay resistance x capacitor.

### 2.3 How the best drum synths expose it

- Elektron Analog Rytm: per-voice analogue machine + sample layer, mixed, optionally overdriven, into a 12 dB multimode filter (LP/BP/HP/notch/peak plus 6 dB modes), then analogue distortion and compression on the master; filter and amp each have full ADSR with a hold stage "to add body"; overdrive is pre-filter (https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/elektron-analog-rytm). BD Hard parameters: Tune, Sweep Time (depth fixed), Snap, Decay, Wave (sine / asymmetric sine / triangle), Hold, Tick level, Level; BD Classic swaps Snap/Tick for Sweep Depth and a combined Transient (0 to 55 selectable tick/noise) (https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/657159/elektron-analog-rytm.html?page=95). Note the three body waveforms and the hold-before-decay: both are cheap and audible.
- Roland TR-8S: ACB models 808, 909, 707, 727, 606 "component-by-component", 11 ACB kick variants; per-instrument hidden parameters (kick Attack, snare Snappy, tom Colour) plus an assignable CTRL; any track can be ACB or sample with its own tune, envelope, filter with AD envelope, bit reduction and stereo spread (https://www.musicradar.com/reviews/roland-tr-8s-rhythm-performer; Roland's ACB description: https://rolandindonesia.com/what-is-analog-circuit-behavior-acb).
- Sonic Charge Microtonic: per channel, Oscillator (sine/triangle/saw, 20 Hz to 20 kHz) with pitch modulation modes Decaying (plus or minus 96 st), Sine (LFO at low rates, FM-metallic at high rates for hats and cowbells) and Random (per-trigger random pitch at zero rate, filtered-noise band at high rates, plus or minus 48 st); a Noise generator with multimode filter, its own envelope with selectable shapes and a stereo mode using two uncorrelated sources; a Mix stage with distortion and EQ; velocity routed to osc level, noise level and pitch-mod amount (https://manualsdir.com/manuals/349731/sonic-charge-microtonic.html?page=4; https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/349731/sonic-charge-microtonic.html?page=15). This is the minimal "pro" drum voice: one tone, one noise, each with its own filter and envelope shape, plus mix-stage drive.
- Digitone II FM Drum: 3-op FM body into a wavefolder, transient section mixing noise with PCM attacks, independent hold-decay on body and transient; became the reviewer's default drum machine (https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/elektron-digitone-ii).
- Kick 2: pitch and amplitude as freely drawn curves, click layers, distortion options (https://musicradar.com/reviews/tech/sonic-academy-kick-2-640392; https://www.audiotent.com/blogs/production-tips/production-tipsultimate-guide-sonic-academy-kick-2).

What makes a synthesised drum read as "pro", pulling the above together: a separate click/transient layer with its own level; pitch envelope with a very fast initial segment and a slow tail (or two envelopes); a hold stage before decay; asymmetric body waveform options; drive placed before a filter; for hats, a set of six inharmonic square partials plus band-pass/high-pass with a resonant HF peak near 10 kHz rather than filtered white noise; and a master drive/compressor that is itself good (the TR-8S criticism).

## 3. Effects that make the difference

### 3.1 Reverb

- Dattorro's plate (Effect Design Part 1): the canonical input diffusion + figure-eight tank with modulated allpasses; paper at https://ccrma.stanford.edu/%7Edattorro/EffectDesignPart1.pdf; open implementations include https://github.com/el-visio/dattorro-verb and Valley Audio's Plateau (https://valleyaudio.github.io/rack/plateau).
- FDN theory: Julius Smith's chapters on FDN reverberation, Householder and Hadamard lossless feedback matrices (https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/Householder_Feedback_Matrix.html; https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/pasp/FDN_Reverberation.html; https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/pasp/Choice_Lossless_Feedback_Matrix.html).
- What practitioners say: Sean Costello (Valhalla) says he has built hundreds of algorithms since 1998 and "there is no Platonic Ideal for reverb algorithms"; mystran says pure FDNs are "far from ideal" because the tail is hard to shape and often lifeless, that they need extra early diffusion, that naive Householder matrices did not sound pleasant to him and that matrix and delay lengths must be tuned together; another developer notes modulation sources cost more CPU than the nested allpass structure; frequency-dependent decay is done by replacing an allpass coefficient with a shelf and compensating the feedforward path; hardware designers EQ out bass before the delays, particularly for early reflections (https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=4968720). Valhalla's approach to delays is documented mode by mode (see 3.2).
- Ableton Hybrid Reverb: convolution engine with IR categories and attack/decay/size envelope shaping, plus a pure delay-line algorithmic engine with five algorithms (Dark Hall, Quartz, Shimmer, Tides, Prism) "from clean and creamy to metallic and gong-like", serial/parallel routing, 4-band EQ with slopes to 96 dB/oct and a Pre Algo position, Freeze and Freeze In, and a "Vintage" output degradation that emulates older digital units (https://www.ableton.com/en/packs/hybrid-reverb/).

### 3.2 Delay (tape, BBD, digital)

ValhallaDelay's mode documentation is the best concise spec of what each era needs: Tape (RE-201/301): wow and flutter rate and depth both depend on delay time, EQ varies with delay time, Age sets asperity noise and splice artefacts, Past/Present eras are darker and dirtier with stronger HF oscillation at short delays; BBD: compander artefacts (ducking at high drive), level-dependent noise, smoothed-triangle LFO, limited HF; Digital: Age sets bit depth (50% = 12-bit), era selects frequency response and saturation type; Ghost adds frequency shifting and frequency-dependent diffusion (https://valhalladsp.com/2019/04/16/valhalladelay-the-mode-control).

### 3.3 Saturation

See 1.4 for the ADAA mechanics. The practical points: use a shaper with a closed-form antiderivative (x/sqrt(1+x^2) per Vicanek) or tanh with its antiderivative; first-order ADAA plus 2x is a good default, 4x makes a hard clipper "practically a non-issue" (https://www.vicanek.de/articles/AADistortion.pdf). Placement matters as much as curve: Drift puts asymmetric clipping pre-filter and a second stage near +6 dB, with post-filter distortion affecting resonance (https://cdm.link/2023/05/inside-ableton-drift/); Analog Rytm places overdrive pre-filter per voice and analogue distortion on the master (https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/elektron-analog-rytm).

### 3.4 Glue compressor (SSL G bus)

The SSL G is a VCA compressor with ratios 2:1, 4:1, 10:1 and a variable knee; attack stepped 0.1 to 30 ms, release stepped 100 ms to 1.2 s plus Auto; threshold is adjusted per ratio so switching ratios keeps gain reduction similar; the UA emulation adds a 12 dB/oct sidechain HPF to 500 Hz, a mix knob and a headroom control (4 to 28 dB, default 16); reviewers describe "a glue that really helps tie the whole mix together" (https://www.recordingmag.com/resources/featured-reviews/universal-audio-ssl-4000-g-bus-compressor-plug-in-collection/). Feedback vs feedforward detection is not stated in that review; the classic hardware G-series is widely described as a feedback-topology VCA design, which is what gives it the programme-dependent, soft onset (general audio-engineering knowledge rather than from a fetched source). The master glue compressor should expose exactly these: 2/4/10 ratio, 0.1 to 30 ms attack, auto release, sidechain HPF, mix.

### 3.5 Transient shaper

Two envelope followers with different timings, one fast attack/fast release and one slower attack/fast release; their difference, scaled by the amount, drives the gain; attack shaping and release shaping swap the arrangement; tuning the time constants is source-dependent (snare needs faster than kick, guitar needs smoother), and multiplying two wideband signals aliases unless you oversample or use an RMS-style follower (https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=5067014; SPL's original description https://www.scopeusers.com/ScopeRise/issue_09/spl_td.htm).

### 3.6 True-peak limiting

ITU BS.1770 true-peak metering requires at least 4x oversampling (48 to 192 kHz) using a 48-tap, 4-phase FIR; typical programme hides 0.5 to 1.5 dB of inter-sample overshoot above sample peak; a 4x meter can still under-read by up to 0.688 dB on a tone near half the sample rate; lossy codecs reconstruct samples that can land above the original true peak, and AES TD1008 notes overshoot grows as bitrate falls; ceilings: EBU R128 -1 dBTP, Spotify -1 dBTP (-2 dBTP if louder than -14 LUFS), Apple about -1 dBTP, Netflix -2 dBTP (https://www.forasoft.com/learn/audio-for-video/articles-audio/true-peak-dbtp-inter-sample). The limiter should detect on a 4x upsampled sidechain and default to a -1 dBTP ceiling.

## 4. Mastering-grade output chain and why a learning app benefits

- Standards: EBU R128 targets -23 LUFS integrated plus or minus 0.5 LU (plus or minus 1 LU for live), max -1 dBTP; BS.1770 gating is -70 LUFS absolute and -10 LU relative; streaming targets are -14 LUFS (Spotify, YouTube, Tidal, Amazon), -16 LUFS (Apple Music), -18 LUFS (Qobuz); YouTube and Tidal normalise downward only (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EBU_R_128; EBU docs https://tech.ebu.ch/loudness and https://tech.ebu.ch/docs/r/r128-2014.pdf; SOS on how loud to master for streaming: https://www.soundonsound.com/sound-advice/q-how-loud-should-master-tracks-intended-streaming).
- Dither: needed only when reducing word length (e.g. exporting 16-bit); noise shaping pushes the dither noise above 10 kHz where hearing is less sensitive (overview: https://www.bhphotovideo.com/explora/pro-audio/tips-and-solutions/dither-me-timbers-an-honest-look-at-the-mystery-of-dither). Inside a float engine you do not dither; you dither once at export.
- Why a learning app benefits (our argument, built on the numbers above): (a) learners judge their work on phones where the OS or app may apply up to 3 dB of decode-side limiting if there is no headroom (Forasoft, above), so an uncontrolled output chain makes beginner mixes sound worse than they are; (b) a built-in LUFS/true-peak meter turns "why is my track quieter than Spotify" into a lesson with a number attached; (c) exporting at -14 LUFS / -1 dBTP by default means every learner export survives normalisation untouched, which is a demonstrable, marketable promise ("what you hear in the lesson is what Spotify will play"); (d) a correct glue compressor and limiter are the two things the TR-8S and Digitakt II reviews above say make the difference between "digital" and "immense".

## 5. Performance budget on the web

- Render quantum: 128 frames; Chrome's own guidance is a budget of about 3 ms per quantum at 44.1 kHz (2.67 ms at 48 kHz), and exceeding it shifts the next callback and glitches; WebAssembly is recommended in the worklet to avoid JIT and GC in the audio path; a worker + SharedArrayBuffer ring-buffer pattern exists but in Chrome the worker runs at lower priority than the audio render thread (https://developer.chrome.com/blog/audio-worklet-design-pattern). Firefox's AudioWorklet notes: https://hacks.mozilla.org/?p=46115.
- Realistic budget (our estimate): plan to use at most 40 to 50% of the quantum on a mid-range phone, i.e. about 1.0 to 1.3 ms of compute per 128 frames at 48 kHz, so the UI thread, GC pauses and thermal throttling do not cause dropouts. At 2x oversampling that is 256 internal samples per quantum. For 8 layers with, say, 24 total voices, that leaves roughly 160 to 200 ns per voice-sample on a desktop and a fraction of that on a 2020-era Android phone, which is enough for a BLEP oscillator pair, a ZDF ladder with one tanh per stage and ADAA drive, but not enough for 4x global oversampling plus Newton iterations on every voice. Keep 2x global; spend ADAA on nonlinearities; iterate the filter solver at most once or twice with a good initial guess; reserve 4x for the master limiter sidechain only.
- SIMD: wasm fixed-width SIMD is supported in Chrome 91+, Firefox 89+, Safari and iOS Safari 16.4+, 96.5% global usage (https://caniuse.com/wasm-simd). Drift's DFM1 was rewritten for "vectorized rendering of four voices at once" (https://cdm.link/2023/05/inside-ableton-drift/), which is exactly the f32x4 layout to use in Rust: process four voices per lane, not four samples. Relaxed SIMD (FMA) support is narrower; feature-detect it (https://github.com/sonos/tract/pull/2199 shows a real-world madd_f32x4 use).
- Latency: the best measured end-to-end numbers found are Jeff Kaufman's (macOS, 2021): Chrome ~67 ms default, ~19 ms with latencyHint 0; Firefox ~55 ms default, ~14 ms low-latency; he also found Chrome's reported latency values unreliable (https://www.jefftk.com/p/browser-audio-latency). The spec hooks are AudioContext.baseLatency and outputLatency (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioContext/baseLatency) and latencyHint on construction (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioContextOptions). Superpowered maintains a browser latency measurement tool (https://github.com/superpoweredSDK/WebBrowserAudioLatencyMeasurement). Apple developer forums document iOS Chrome tabs with extreme AudioContext latency (https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/699192). No 2025 cross-browser table was found; treat 10 to 20 ms as achievable on desktop Chrome with latencyHint "interactive" or 0, plan for 20 to 40 ms on iOS Safari, and build the step sequencer to schedule ahead with AudioContext.currentTime rather than relying on event-to-sound latency.
- Cross-origin isolation: SharedArrayBuffer (needed for worker-based designs and used by the NAM web player) requires COOP/COEP headers; without them "many AudioWorklet-based paths fail" (https://www.tone3000.com/blog/introducing-the-nam-web-player).

## 6. New or notable in 2024 to 2025: what "future-ready" should mean

- MPE and per-note expression: MPE was added to the MIDI spec in 2018, assigns each note its own channel (15 usable voices with a master channel), carries per-note bend, continuous pressure and a timbre dimension; MIDI 2.0 (2020) includes MPE as a subset; SOS considers it a success and a matured standard (https://www.soundonsound.com/sound-advice/mpe-midi-polyphonic-expression). Drift, Collision and Granulator III are all MPE instruments, Push 3 is "MPE-enabled standalone" (https://www.engadget.com/the-new-ableton-push-is-an-mpe-enabled-standalone-music-production-powerhouse-081118260.html; review https://www.musicradar.com/reviews/ableton-push-3), and the Digitone II review's main sound complaint is the lack of polyphonic aftertouch (https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/elektron-digitone-ii). Expressive E's Osmose is the reference controller (https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/expressive-e-osmose). For a touch-screen learning app, per-note pressure/slide from multi-touch is a native fit.
- Microtuning: ODDSound MTS-ESP is the de facto plugin tuning bus (client library: https://github.com/ODDSound/MTS-ESP; background https://cdm.link/inside-mts-esp-the-new-tuning-tool-from-oddsound-and-aphex-twin/); Live 12 ships tuning systems and Granulator III documents setting pitch bend range to 48 st so MPE bend and Live 12 tunings work (https://roberthenke.com/technology/granulator3.html). In a web engine the equivalent is a per-note frequency table (Scala/.tun import) rather than MTS-ESP IPC.
- Neural modelling in wasm: feasible for small models. TONE3000's NAM Web Player runs the NeuralAmpModelerCore C++ engine compiled to WebAssembly inside an AudioWorkletProcessor, processing "small blocks of audio sample by sample"; it "needs a modern processor, such as Apple Silicon or recent Intel/AMD", older or low-power devices "may not play back correctly", and a pre-rendered fallback is in development (https://www.tone3000.com/blog/introducing-the-nam-web-player); there is also a Rust port, NeuralAmpModeler-rs (https://docs.rs/crate/NeuralAmpModeler-rs/latest/source/README.md). A 2025 arXiv paper on designing neural synthesizers for low-latency interaction exists but could not be fetched (https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.11562). Verdict: a "nano" NAM-style WaveNet for a master saturation or amp flavour is plausible on desktop as an optional high-quality mode; it is not a sensible default on phones alongside 8 layers.
- Spectral and resynthesis: Serum 2's Spectral oscillator is the marquee 2025 feature (https://static.xferrecords.com/Serum%202%20What's%20New.pdf; https://musictech.com/reviews/software-instruments/xfer-records-serum-2-review); Pigments has a Harmonic (additive) engine (https://www.musicradar.com/music-tech/synths/arturia-pigments-review).
- Stem separation on device: htdemucs runs in-browser via onnxruntime-web on WebGPU, about 126 MB mixed-precision, 21-piece split, roughly 22x real time on a Radeon RX 7600 (so a 3-minute song in about 8 seconds) (https://huggingface.co/monteslu/htdemucs-web-onnx); a smaller SCNet ONNX core for 4-stem separation also exists (https://huggingface.co/t4t2k1m/yt-precount-scnet-tran-onnx). This is an offline, GPU-dependent feature, not an audio-thread one, but it is a strong pedagogical hook (pull the kick out of a reference track and compare to yours).
- Standalone instrument design cues: Ableton Move ships Drift, Wavetable and Drum Rack plus Drum Sampler and Simpler, one insert and one send per track, four-track limit, randomised starting kit, reviewers call the sounds "fantastic" and "a diverse but cohesive sound palette" (https://musictech.com/reviews/controllers/ableton-move-review/; CDM hands-on https://cdm.link/ableton-move-hands-on/). OP-XY: eight engines (Axis FM, Dissolve swarm, Epiano, Organ, Prism VA, Simple, Wavetable, Hardsync), each with exactly four macro controls, tape aux, six FX, per-step components (https://www.perfectcircuit.com/signal/teenage-engineering-op-xy-review; SOS https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/teenage-engineering-op-xy). Digitakt II: stereo sampling, Werp/Stretch/Repitch/Grid machines, per-track bit reduction/overdrive/SRR, send reverb/delay/chorus, master overdrive and compressor with per-track bypass for sidechain-style setups, filter set Multi-mode / Lowpass 4 / EQ / Comb / Legacy (https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/elektron-digitakt-ii). The common pattern across all three: few engines, four big knobs each, a strong master drive + compressor, and a comb/physical-modelling filter mode.

## 7. Cost and audible payoff per technique

Effort is our estimate for a competent Rust DSP dev who already has the engine described: S = days, M = 1 to 3 weeks, L = a month or more. "Hear" is what a learner should notice in an A/B.

| Technique | Effort | What a learner will hear in an A/B |
|---|---|---|
| ADAA (1st order) on every drive/crush/clip stage, with sqrt(1+x^2) or tanh antiderivative | S | Drive and crush stop "fizzing"; cranked kicks and bass get harmonics, not metallic hash. Biggest payoff per hour. |
| Per-voice seeded drift + component tolerance (osc pitch, filter cutoff, env times), "serial number" saved in preset | S | Chords beat slowly, repeated notes differ, pads sound "alive"; the Drift/Diva effect. |
| JP-8000 detune curve + centre/side mix law + HPF at fundamental + random phases for chord/lead unison | S | Supersaws stop sounding like a comb and open up; the "sweet spot" is obvious. |
| ZDF ladder with per-stage tanh, 1 to 2 Newton steps, plus a Sallen-Key (MS-20) and 2-pole SVF (SEM) model | M | Resonance stays put during fast filter envelopes and at high cutoff; MS-20 "screams", SEM stays smooth, ladder loses bass at high Q. |
| Mip-mapped band-limited wavetable oscillator (per-octave FFT tables, Hermite read, crossfade) | M | No aliasing on high leads; bright wavetables stay clean. |
| 808-style kick: shaped pulse click, 6 ms Q-kick pitch bump, slow "sigh", feedback-path decay, hold stage, asymmetric sine/tri body | M | Kick has a real attack and a pitch that breathes; retriggers do not machine-gun. |
| 808-style hat/cymbal: six square partials at the measured frequencies, BP 3.4k/7.1k, resonant HP near 10.5k, swing-VCA clip | S to M | Hats go from "filtered noise" to metallic; the single most recognisable drum upgrade. |
| Drum voice: separate noise layer with own filter and envelope shape, pitch-mod modes (decay / sine-FM / random) | M | Snares and percs get snap and body independently; FM-metal toms and cowbells become possible. |
| Modal/comb resonator (Collision-style 2-resonator, or Digitone-style keytracked comb) as a filter mode or layer | M | Plucks, mallets, "piano" from a click; the physical-modelling sound reviewers call "surprisingly convincing". |
| Dattorro plate + modulated FDN hall with frequency-dependent decay, pre-delay EQ, freeze | M to L | Reverb stops ringing/metallic, tails are smooth, kicks do not mud it up. |
| Tape/BBD delay model: delay-time-dependent wow/flutter and EQ, compander ducking, level-dependent noise | M | Dub delays feel like hardware; repeats darken and wobble. |
| SSL-style glue comp (2/4/10, 0.1 to 30 ms, auto release, SC HPF, mix) + 4x true-peak limiter at -1 dBTP + LUFS meter | M | Whole mix "glues" and gets loud without pumping or codec clipping; export matches Spotify. |
| Transient shaper (dual follower difference, RMS-style detector) | S | Kicks punch through or sit back with one knob. |
| FM feedback with two-sample averaging and through-zero option; phase distortion (CZ) mode | S | Operator feedback goes saw to noise musically; PD gives filter-sweep tones with no filter. |
| Granular layer (Cloud mode: up to 20 grains, per-grain variation, grain-boundary parameter changes) | M to L | Textures and pads from any sample; chorused clouds. |
| Spectral resynthesis oscillator | L | Serum 2's headline sound; probably not worth it before the above. |
| Neural (NAM-style) saturation as desktop-only "HQ" mode | L | Subtle; marketing value more than learner-audible value. Skip for now. |
| MPE / per-note pressure and slide from touch, microtuning table | S to M | Expressive leads; not an A/B "sound" feature but a "future-ready" checkbox reviewers look for. |
| f32x4 four-voices-per-lane SIMD refactor | M | Not audible; buys the headroom to afford the rest on phones. |

## The twelve things that would make this engine audibly world class, in order of payoff per effort

1. ADAA on every nonlinearity (drive, crush, filter tanh stages, limiter clip). Small effort, removes the single most recognisable "cheap digital" artefact (https://www.vicanek.de/articles/AADistortion.pdf; https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jatin/Notebooks/adaa.html).
2. Seeded per-voice drift and tolerance on oscillators, filter and envelopes, saved with the preset (https://cdm.link/2023/05/inside-ableton-drift/; https://u-he.com/products/diva/).
3. 808-measured hi-hat and cymbal partial set with the 3.4k/7.1k band-passes and 10.5k resonant high-pass (https://speech.di.uoa.gr/ICMC-SMC-2014/images/VOL_2/1453.pdf).
4. JP-8000 detune law for unison on chord and lead (https://gist.github.com/audionerd/fe50790b7601cba65ddd855caffb05ad).
5. Kick rebuilt around the Werner/Abel/Smith 808 structure: shaped-pulse click, 6 ms attack Q-bump, slow sigh, feedback-path decay, hold stage, asymmetric body waveforms (https://dafx14.fau.de/papers/dafx14_kurt_james_werner_a_physically_informed,_ci.pdf; https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/657159/elektron-analog-rytm.html?page=95).
6. Master chain done properly: SSL-topology glue (2/4/10, auto release, SC HPF, mix) and a 4x-oversampled true-peak limiter at -1 dBTP with an integrated LUFS meter and -14 LUFS export default (https://www.recordingmag.com/resources/featured-reviews/universal-audio-ssl-4000-g-bus-compressor-plug-in-collection/; https://www.forasoft.com/learn/audio-for-video/articles-audio/true-peak-dbtp-inter-sample; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EBU_R_128). This is the lesson in the TR-8S and Digitakt II reviews: the drive and compressor decide whether the box sounds "digital" or "immense".
7. Three filter personalities instead of one: keep the ZDF ladder, add an MS-20 Sallen-Key and a 2-pole SEM SVF, each with the nonlinearity in the right place, plus asymmetric pre-filter drive with a second stage near +6 dB (https://www.native-instruments.com/fileadmin/ni_media/downloads/pdf/VAFilterDesign_2.1.0.pdf; https://cdm.link/2023/05/inside-ableton-drift/; https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=4921167).
8. Drum voices get a Microtonic-style independent noise layer (own filter, own envelope shape) and pitch-mod modes decay / sine-FM / random (https://manualsdir.com/manuals/349731/sonic-charge-microtonic.html?page=4; https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/349731/sonic-charge-microtonic.html?page=15).
9. A modulated plate (Dattorro) and a tuned FDN hall with frequency-dependent decay and pre-delay low cut, replacing or upgrading the current reverb send (https://ccrma.stanford.edu/%7Edattorro/EffectDesignPart1.pdf; https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/Householder_Feedback_Matrix.html; https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=4968720).
10. A keytracked comb / two-resonator modal mode (Collision-lite) available to chord, lead and sampler layers for plucks, mallets and "piano" (https://www.ableton.com/en/packs/collision/; https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/elektron-digitone-ii; https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/Karplus_Strong_Algorithms.html).
11. Tape/BBD delay model with delay-time-dependent wow, flutter and EQ, and compander ducking (https://valhalladsp.com/2019/04/16/valhalladelay-the-mode-control).
12. Mip-mapped band-limited wavetable oscillator plus FM feedback averaging / through-zero and a CZ phase-distortion mode on the chord and lead synth, with a transient shaper on the drum bus (https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=5269992; https://community.vcvrack.com/t/frequency-modulation-feedback-behaviour/9981; https://musicradar.com/news/tech/what-is-phase-distortion-synthesis-618081; https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=5067014).

Enablers that are not audible but make the twelve affordable on phones: a four-voices-per-lane f32x4 SIMD layout (https://caniuse.com/wasm-simd; the Drift DFM1 precedent at https://cdm.link/2023/05/inside-ableton-drift/), staying at 2x global oversampling and reserving 4x for the limiter sidechain, a ~1 ms per-quantum compute ceiling (https://developer.chrome.com/blog/audio-worklet-design-pattern), and scheduling the sequencer ahead on AudioContext time because measured browser output latency is 14 to 67 ms depending on settings (https://www.jefftk.com/p/browser-audio-latency).

Deliberately deprioritised for this engine: spectral resynthesis (large, Serum 2's headline but not a learner-audible basic), neural saturation in wasm (works, but TONE3000 says it needs a modern CPU and is building a pre-rendered fallback, https://www.tone3000.com/blog/introducing-the-nam-web-player), and on-device stem separation (strong teaching feature but a GPU/offline job, https://huggingface.co/monteslu/htdemucs-web-onnx). MPE/per-note touch expression and a microtuning table are cheap "future-ready" checkboxes worth adding once the audible list is done (https://www.soundonsound.com/sound-advice/mpe-midi-polyphonic-expression; https://github.com/ODDSound/MTS-ESP).

Marketing lines the evidence supports: "filters solved the way u-he and Ableton solve them (zero-delay feedback, nonlinear per stage)"; "drums built from the measured 808 circuits, not filtered noise"; "anti-aliased saturation everywhere, so drive adds harmonics, not hash"; "every voice is a slightly different voice card"; "a true-peak-safe, loudness-aware master, so your export sounds the same on Spotify as in the lesson". Avoid claiming "component-level circuit simulation" unless it is actually implemented; the Xils thread shows how quickly that claim gets tested.
