# CCD School engine 2: what we build, why people will hear it, and what we can say

Written 5 Nov 2026 for Param, before any engine code changes. It builds on `ccd/engine-sound.md` (3 Nov) and two research reports with sources for every claim: `design/research/engine-sound.md` (sound) and `design/research/instrument-ui.md` (instrument screens). The build status of each item is just below; every number in it comes from `engine/tests/sound.rs` (run `cargo test --release --test sound -- --nocapture` to see them).

## Build status

CPU is from `engine/tests/bench.rs` (a full eight-layer song, two of each synth, analysis on, 128-frame blocks, best of five runs, native) on a shared build machine, so take the worst-block figures as rough.

- **1. ADAA drive:** done (ac9d38d). Heavy drive on high notes 13 to 23 dB cleaner.
- **2. Voice cards:** done (f10fa02).
- **3. 808 hats and cymbals:** done (b4c598a).
- **4. Supersaw:** done (b4c598a).
- **5. 808 kick:** done. Attack bump, sigh, shaped click, hold and second ring (loop rounds 1 and 2), and three body shapes on a new second page of pad knobs ("body": round, tight, hard; an anti-aliased saturator on the body, level-matched): overtones against the fundamental in the tail -65.8 dB round, -17.3 dB tight, -8.5 dB hard, first 100 ms within 0.3 dB of each other. Clip: "kick bodies". The second pad page is its own wire block with room for eight knobs per pad, so the first four keep their indices in saved songs.
- **6. Master chain:** done. The glue has a ratio switch (2, 4, 10 to 1; static gain reduction within 0.05 dB of the curve at each), auto release (two envelopes, kept), a fourth-order high-pass on what it listens to (90 Hz by default, off at the bottom of its knob) and a dry/compressed mix. Pumping on a kick under a held chord (the chord's average dip per beat): old glue 1.32 dB, new at 4:1 hearing everything 1.44 dB, at 4:1 with the 90 Hz cut 0.98 dB, the new default (2:1, 90 Hz) 0.66 dB; half mix cuts 10:1 pumping by a third. The true-peak limiter at -1 dBTP is unchanged. A loudness meter to ITU-R BS.1770-4 / EBU R 128 (K-weighting, momentary, short-term, gated integrated) reads the EBU Tech 3341 cases 1 to 5 within 0.02 LU and BS.1770's one-channel 997 Hz figure at -3.01; the old K-weighting read 0.04 dB low (the high-pass numerator was normalised twice) and is fixed for the layer meters too. The master reports momentary, short-term and integrated loudness to the mix screen. Studio has a wav export (offline render through the same engine, -14 LUFS integrated, or -9 LUFS "loud wav" with shift held), landing within 0.03 LU of target with the true peak at or under -1.0 dBTP on the standard meter. Clip: "glued song" (the whole song with the glue pushed hard). CPU: 21.4% before, 21.6% after.
- **7. Three filter characters:** done, on bass, chords and lead ("character": warm, scream, smooth; plus "filter drive"). Warm is the ladder as it was (24 dB per octave; at res 0.6 the bass drops 2.9 dB and the cutoff rises 11.8 dB). Scream is a zero-delay-feedback Korg35 (MS-20 style) 2-pole with a soft clipper in the loop (12 dB per octave, bass unchanged with resonance, +8.3 dB at the cutoff, self-oscillates at the cutoff frequency at full resonance). Smooth is an SEM-style 2-pole state-variable filter (12 dB per octave, bass unchanged, +16.8 dB at the cutoff, never self-oscillates: -180 dB a second after a click at full resonance). Filter drive: an asymmetric anti-aliased saturator before the filter (2nd harmonic -17 to -28 dB on a sine, level within 0.3 dB) and a second stage after it taking up to +6 dB; both bypassed exactly at zero, so warm with no drive is the old sound and no existing clip moved. The three sit at the same loudness (-16.6 dB) on a resonant bass line. Full resonance with the fastest filter envelope stays bounded (peak 0.44 to 1.42), and every synth, character, knob end and the keyboard's ends stay finite. Clips: "scream filter", "smooth filter". CPU: 21.3% before, 21.5% after at the defaults; 23.0% with every synth on scream with its drive up.
- **8. Drum voices with tone and noise layers:** done, on the pad's second page ("noise", "snap", "color", "sweep", "wobble", "random"; "sound" on the perc pads). Every pad is a tone layer and a noise layer: the balance knob (0.5 is the pad as designed), the noise layer's own envelope (snap: a quarter to four times as long) and its own filter (color: two octaves either way); the tone layer keeps its envelope on decay and, where it has overtones, its own filters (the 808 band-passes on the metal, the cowbell's band-pass). Snare: body only puts the snap band at -103 dB, snap only puts the body band at -36 dB; snap 0.15 against 0.85 takes the noise from 55 ms to 325 ms to fall 30 dB; color -0.6 to +0.6 moves the noise from 3.3 to 7.1 kHz. Tom: sweep 0 / 0.5 / 1 starts the hit 1.00x / 1.33x / 2.10x its settled pitch; wobble at full swings it 197 cents (0 off); random at full spreads eight hits over 117 cents and 5.3 dB (5 cents and 0.25 dB off, which is the free-running second oscillator). Metallic percussion on the perc pads: an 808-style cowbell (540 and 800 Hz squares through a band-pass, two-stage decay; partial ratios 1, 2.99, 3.36) and a struck metal bar (modes 1, 2.756, 5.404, ring-modulated; ratios 1, 2.68, 7.38). At the defaults every pad sounds as before (the default clips render the same numbers). Clips: "snare layers", "metal percussion". CPU: 22.1% for the engine before items 7 and 8, 23.1% after both.
- **9. A smoother hall and a plate:** done, chosen on the master ("space": hall or plate; "verb time": 0.4 to 8 s, 2.4 by default). The hall keeps its eight-line network and now drifts every line slowly (0.11 to 0.47 Hz, a quarter of a millisecond) and splits each line's loss at 6 kHz (two-band absorbent filter), so the time knob is honest through the middle of the band and the top dies first. The plate is Dattorro's 1997 design (the paper's delays and output taps scaled to 48 kHz, modulated tank allpasses, damping in the tank). Measured (RT60 from the Schroeder integral, T20): set to 2.4 s, the old hall read 2.38 / 2.24 / 1.59 / 1.13 s at 250 Hz / 1 kHz / 4 kHz / 8 kHz with no way to change it; the new hall 2.55 / 2.33 / 1.65 / 1.17 s, and 1.05 s and 4.70 s when set to 1 and 5; the plate 2.48 / 2.45 / 2.11 / 1.76 s. Smoothness (flatness of one long look at the tail, decay removed, each bin against its neighbours; ringing modes pull it down): old hall 0.258, new hall 0.406, plate 0.419. Left/right correlation in the tail: old 0.116, hall 0.092, plate 0.015. Echo density fills in after 200 ms (old), 220 ms (hall), 420 ms (plate: its taps start sparse). Levels matched to the old hall within 0.3 dB on the same send. The fx instrument's own reverb is the new hall too. Clips: "hall" (the reverb clip re-rendered), "plate"; the song clips re-rendered. CPU: 22.4% for the engine before item 6, 22.8% after item 9 (same run).
- **10. Resonator voices:** done, on the chord synth and the lead ("voice": synth, pluck, mallet, keys; "ring": how long it rings at middle c, 2 s by default, low notes longer; "bright": the strike). A resonator is a Karplus-Strong loop tuned to the exact period: whole-sample delay, a symmetric 3-tap loss filter (exactly one sample, so it never pulls the pitch) and a first-order Thiran allpass for the fraction; the loop gain is set from the decay asked for at the fundamental after the loss filter's own loss. Pluck: a noise burst one period long. Mallet (the two-resonator mode): the bar's mode and one four times up, each struck at its own mode, the upper dying three times faster, plus a click for hardness. Keys: a felt hammer on two strings 1.2 cents either side, unequal, so they beat as a piano's unison does. Pitch, notes 24 to 108 in steps of 6 (autocorrelation): pluck within 0.7 cents, mallet within 0.8, keys within 2.2. Decay at the fundamental against what is asked: pluck 3.94 / 2.78 / 1.95 / 1.38 / 0.98 s at notes 36 / 48 / 60 / 72 / 84 (asked 3.94 / 2.78 / 1.97 / 1.39 / 0.98), mallet 3.03 / 2.44 / 1.97 / 1.60 / 1.30 (asked 2.98 / 2.42 / 1.97 / 1.60 / 1.30); keys beat, so one time does not describe them. Mallet: the upper mode +3.3 dB against the note at the strike, -22.8 dB 800 ms on. Levels within 0.8 dB of the synth voice on middle c. With the voice on synth nothing changed (no clip moved). Clips (after only, there is no before): "plucked strings", "mallet", "keys"; /sound plays them without the then/now switch and the blind test leaves them out. CPU: 22.0% before, 22.5% after at the defaults; 18.4% with every chord and lead voice on keys (two resonators cost less than the supersaw stack).
- **11. Tape and analogue echo:** done, chosen on the master ("echo": clean, tape, analogue, tape by default; "repeats": the feedback). Clean is the old echo, its code untouched, so it is the "before" in every measurement. Tape: wow (0.55 Hz), flutter (6.5 Hz) and a slow random wander, all a share of the delay time (0.315% peak to peak at 150, 375 and 750 ms: 22.7, 56.7 and 113.4 samples), so the pitch wobble grows with the delay as a tape's does (up to 2.7, 6.8 and 13.6 cents); each repeat goes back through a record-head saturation and loses top and bottom. Analogue (bucket brigade): a triangle clock wobble (0.300% of the delay at every time, up to 3.1 cents at 750 ms) and darker two-pole repeats. Per repeat the top falls 8.3 dB against the middle on clean, 8.5 dB on tape, 10.7 dB on analogue. A loud tone's first repeat: third harmonic at -133.8 dB clean, -19.8 dB tape, -23.7 dB analogue. Both duck: repeats sit 5.8 dB (tape) and 5.4 dB (analogue) lower while you play into them than in the gaps (clean 0.0). Clip: "tape echo" (the echo clip re-rendered); the song clips re-rendered. CPU: 21.1% for the engine before item 6, 22.7% after item 11 (same run).
- **12. Wavetable, feedback fm and phase distortion:** done, on the chord synth and the lead ("osc": classic, table, feedback, phase; the wave knob is each one's morph). Table: eight single-cycle frames (sine, triangle, saw, square, quarter pulse, vowel, bright, hollow) built from their harmonics by inverse FFT, one copy per octave holding only the harmonics under 40 kHz at the voice's 96 kHz (so whatever passes the base Nyquist is in the decimator's stopband), read with four-point Hermite and crossfaded between frames; built once (the whole set in under 20 ms native) when the first synth is made. Feedback: a sine pushed by its own last two outputs, averaged (the DX7's operator feedback). Phase: the CZ saw, a cosine read through a bent phase, bent by the wave knob and the filter envelope, the bend capped on high notes so its steep part lasts at least four samples. Aliasing (energy off the harmonics, audible band, notes 36 to 108): the classic saw (before) -82 dB at note 36 falling to -49 dB at 108; table saw frame worst -70.5, table bright frame worst -56.5 (at note 36, the fullest table), feedback saw-like worst -60.8, phase bent far worst -71.4. Feedback goes from sine (overtones -39 dB of the whole) to saw-like (-5.6 dB) to noise (energy between the harmonics +1.1 dB); phase distortion's overtones rise -39.9 / -27.6 / -17.8 / -14.5 dB as the bend goes 0 / 0.4 / 0.8 / 1. Levels within 1.8 dB of the classic saw. The supersaw stack stays on classic only. Classic is unchanged (no clip moved). Clips (after only): "wavetable", "feedback fm", "phase distortion". CPU: 21.9% for the engine before item 6, 22.6% after all twelve (same machine, best of five); 18.5% with every chord and lead on the wavetable (no supersaw stack).

All twelve items are built. What only an ear can judge, and has not been judged yet: whether each "after" is better than its "before" (Param's blind a/b, per `school-process.md`), the three filter characters' voicing at high resonance, the kick bodies' and the snare layers' defaults, the plate's damping, how much wow is too much on the tape echo, and the resonator voices' strike sounds. Nothing here claims to match any product.

## After engine 2: touch and hear

The "touch and hear" stream from `design/compare.md` (items 1, 2, 3, 4 and 13). Names are Ableton Live 12's where Live has one.

- **1. Drag on the picture:** done. In the studio and the lesson shape steps the filter picture's knee drags (left and right is cutoff, the knee follows the finger on the 20 Hz to 20 kHz axis; up and down is resonance) and the envelope's corners drag (attack peak, decay corner on the bass, release end, a drum hit's length; half the width is four times longer or shorter). The knobs follow, one drag is one undo step, the page never scrolls under the finger (pointer capture, touch-action none), and the knobs stay the way in for the keyboard and screen readers. Shape steps only drag the knobs the lesson names. Tests: `tests/shapes.test.ts` (a corner dragged to a time lands its knob on that time, for lead, chords, bass and drum pads).
- **2. Drag to modulate:** done. Chords and lead have two sources, the lfo and the filter env; the bass has its filter env; drums, fx and the sampler have none yet. Drag a source chip onto any knob of the instrument to route it (the engine's new modulation slots, eight per layer, `cmd::MOD`), or tap the chip to edit that source: every knob shows its amount (drag up and down, arrow keys, double tap takes it off) or a plus to route it. The knob gets an arc outside its ring in the source's colour, from its value as far as the source takes it (both ways for the lfo); the knob still turns under it (Live 12). Sources stay dark until used (Pigments). The voice's own routes (the lfo with "lfo to" on the filter, the filter envelope's "env" amount) show as the same ring on the cutoff, drawn in shares of the cutoff's travel, and dragging a source onto the cutoff sets them, so the lfo lessons work as before. Engine (`engine/tests/modulation.rs`): lfo onto pan by 0.25 swings -7.61 to +7.61 dB left over right, the knob set at 0.25 and 0.75 reads ±7.66 dB; the knob turned to 0.3 under a 0.2 lfo swings 0.02 to 15.93 dB against 0.00 and 16.00 dB for 0.5 and 0.1; the filter envelope onto pan by 0.4 holds at -4.56 dB, the knob at 0.66 reads -4.56 dB, and clearing it puts it back at 0.00 dB; the lfo onto the lead's wave by 0.5 gives the second harmonic -5.4 / -69.4 dB at its ends against -5.4 / -69.8 dB for the knob at 0 and 1; nothing routed is bit-identical (192,000 of 192,000 samples, and the six-instrument test song hashes the same before and after). Routes are saved with the song (`mods` on a layer; older songs load with none). CPU: see the line after item 13.
- **3. A/B:** done, on every instrument in the studio (the a/b key beside the sources: the first press keeps the sound as a and copies it to b; change b, switch, the cross stops comparing and keeps the one you hear) and in every lesson shape step (a is the sound before your tweak, b is now; turning a knob on a goes back to b). Level matched so the louder one can't win: each setting of the part is rendered offline through the same engine (`src/lib/offline.ts`, a second WebAssembly engine as the wav export uses) and measured to BS.1770 (new `ccd_lufs`), and the louder one is trimmed by the difference after its strip, sends included (new `cmd::TRIM`, gliding over 20 ms, not a knob and never saved; matched again when you stop turning). Engine (`engine/tests/ab.rs`): a bass at -16.52 LUFS against the same with drive at -14.96 LUFS; the driven one trimmed by -1.56 dB plays at -16.52 LUFS; a trim of -6 dB plays the part 6.00 dB lower; at 0 dB the trim is out of the signal path, bit-identical. `tests/offline.test.ts` checks the offline render and the match.
- **4. Scope and spectrum:** done. The engine keeps the last 2,048 samples of one part or of the master in a small ring (new `cmd::SCOPE`, `ccd_scope`); the worklet sends them about 30 times a second only while a screen asks. The studio's screen draws the selected part's scope (steadied on a rising zero crossing, a short phosphor fade so a drum hit stays readable); the mix screen draws the master's spectrum (2048-point FFT, 20 Hz to 20 kHz on the filter picture's axis, rising at once and falling 18 dB a second). One canvas per screen on animation frames: 60 a second, 30 on a phone with four cores or fewer or when frames run long; nothing is drawn while nothing plays. Engine (`engine/tests/scope.rs`): the lead's part in the scope crosses zero 211 times a second at 220 Hz with a bass playing under it (the bass alone 23), the master's scope is bit-identical to the output, and with the scope on, the sound is bit-identical. `tests/scope.test.ts`: a full-scale sine reads 0 dB in its band, half the level 6.02 dB lower.
- **13. Match this sound:** done, as a new lesson step kind (`kind: match` in a lesson file: `layer`, the lit `knobs`, and a hidden `target` value for each; compiled by `npm run lessons`, played by `src/components/m1/MatchSound.tsx`). The target is your own part (a kick lesson plays only the kick) with the hidden settings, made by the engine; switch between the target and yours, turn the lit knobs, check. The closeness is 70% sound, 30% settings (`src/lib/matchScore.ts`): both are rendered offline through the same engine and compared by their average spectrum (32 bands, 30 Hz to 16 kHz) and their level over time (10 ms frames), each a mean dB difference. 85% passes. After two tries the knob furthest off is lit and named with which way to go ("try decay: the target's is shorter"); a pass keeps your matched knobs in your song (they ride the lesson's shape into the song, as a shape step's do), and the go key says how close you are until then. In three lessons: the kick designer (tune and decay), your first bass line (cutoff and decay), shape your synth (attack and release on the lead). Calibrated in `tests/match.test.ts` through the real engine: identical settings score 100; a kick far off scores 12 (spectrum 3.2 dB, envelope 14.6 dB off), nearer 64, close 91; a bass 46 against 96; a lead 41 against 97. Not done: a gym drill (the ear gym's drills are their own engine and question format, so it needs a drill type of its own).
- **CPU, items 2 to 4** (`engine/tests/bench.rs`, the full eight-layer song; the build machine was shared with other jobs, load 3 to 6, so before and after ran alternately, three times each, best of five inside each): the engine before item 2, 24.7 / 26.3 / 25.4% of one core; after, nothing routed, 24.4 / 25.9 / 24.8% (no change you can measure: unrouted, untrimmed and with the scope off the new code is three branches a sample); after with every synth layer modulated four ways (the lfo on cutoff and pan, the filter envelope on drive and send), every layer trimmed for a/b and the scope on the master (`CCD_BENCH_MOD=1`), 25.0 / 26.3 / 26.6%. "Match this sound" and the a/b level match render offline on the page's thread, not in the audio callback: a check renders one bar of one part (and the target once), about a tenth of a second in the tests.

## After engine 2: write and play

The "write and play" stream from `design/compare.md` (items 5 to 12). Names are Live 12's where Live has one, else the name most tools share (Logic's, Elektron's, Teenage Engineering's).

- **5. Keys and scales:** done. Live 12's 35 scales (`src/lib/theory.ts`, major and minor first) on the song; the key shows in the studio header and opens a key panel (root, every scale, Live's "fit to scale" for notes outside it). Keys dim the notes outside the key and mark the root, pads and lanes only offer notes in it (any scale, a fourth per row as on Push), the note grid dims rows outside the key and folds to it (Live's highlight and fold). Old major and minor songs load unchanged. Tests: `tests/scales.test.ts`.
- **6. Chord track:** done. One chord per bar with its name (am, f, c, g) and roman numeral (`src/lib/chords.ts`), chords in key for any scale, FL-style progressions (a four-bar progression grows a shorter loop and repeats your parts), "write into chords" (FL's chord stamp). Chord pads, keys, pads, glide and xy light the chord under the playhead. In the key panel's chord track tab and the chord progressions lesson. Tests: `tests/chords.test.ts`.
- **7. Drummer and bass player:** done. Logic's controls (complexity and intensity on one pad, fill, swing; `src/lib/players.ts`), seeded, writing editable notes; the drummer writes the hats part when the song has one, the bass player follows the chord track (root on the one, chord notes, passing notes, slides at high intensity). With an arrangement every section gets its own notes, softer in an intro or break and harder in a drop (sections can now hold their own loop per part). Each drag is one undo step. Tests: `tests/players.test.ts`.
- **8. Transformations:** done on the note grid: Live 12's strum, humanize and velocity shaper, one amount and one key each, on the picked notes or all of them, each press one undo step (`src/lib/transform.ts`, `tests/transform.test.ts`).
- **9. Chance and trig conditions:** done. Hold a step, turn chance (Live 12) or pick a condition (Elektron's 1:2, 2:2, 1:4, 2:4, 3:4, 4:4, fill, not fill); the step shows 50% or 1:2; a fill key shows when a step waits for it. The engine decides at the step (`cmd::EV_COND`, `CHANCE_SEED`, `FILL`; `seq::plays` and the same hash in `src/lib/chance.ts`), counting plays of the part's own loop in an arranged song. Engine (`engine/tests/chance.rs`): 1:2 plays passes 0 2 4 6, 2:2 passes 1 3 5 7, 1:4 passes 0 4; fill steps only while held; 50% chance played 27 of 48 passes, exactly the ones `seq::plays` predicts, the same again with the same seed. CPU 23.4% before and after.
- **10. Punch-in effects:** done. Beat repeat, filter, tape stop and reverb throw (`engine/src/punch.rs`, `cmd::PUNCH`) on the whole mix before the master, held from the studio's mix mode and the dj mixer (mix goal "punch"); how high you press sets the amount. Engine (`engine/tests/punch.rs`): nothing held is bit-identical; 8 ms after letting go the output is the input bit for bit; tape stop set to 0.52 s plays 980 Hz before, 500 Hz halfway, 100 Hz at 90% and silence after (in the engine: -12.8 dB playing, silent 0.15 s after); beat repeat loops a 6000-sample 16th to within 6e-8; the filter at 327 Hz keeps 80 Hz (+0.2 dB) and cuts 5 kHz (-48 dB), the high pass at 1.39 kHz cuts 100 Hz (-45.7 dB) and keeps 8 kHz (+0.1 dB); a reverb throw leaves a -45.7 dB tail where there was none. CPU on a loaded machine, three alternating runs: base 33.2 to 35.3%, now 32.1 to 33.4% (no measurable cost).
- **11. Commit:** done. The EP-133's commit in the studio's section row: copies the loop into the next section and moves you there (your loop becomes section a and its copy b the first time; the last section's commit adds one); back and forward between sections keep each one's own loop. Tests: `tests/commit.test.ts`.
- **12. Per-part length and swing:** done. Each part can loop at its own length (Elektron's per-track length; tiled across the loop, together again at the top) and swing by its own amount (Live's groove per clip); in the studio's steps under "part"; steps past a part's length dim. Old songs play exactly as before. Tests: `tests/partLength.test.ts`.
- **Lessons:** chord track in chord progressions (basics 17); a drummer in rhythm in production (basics 204) and a bass player in bass follows (basics 18), both after you play; scales in scale awareness (producer 222, six of Live's scales); strum, humanize and velocity shaper in midi transformations (producer 278); chance and trig conditions in fills (producer 18); per-part length in polyrhythm (basics 203); commit in scenes (producer 274); punch-in effects in effects performance (dj 14).
- **Not done:** chance and conditions are not in the midi export (Live's chance is per note in a clip; the export plays every note); a part's own length is reset at the top of the song's loop (Elektron with master length set), not free-running; punch-in effects are one at a time and not recorded into the song; the bass player has no acid accent lane of its own (accents are velocity).

## After engine 2: leftovers (engine)

The leftovers from the two streams above (`school-fixes.md` 170 to 184), built 5 Nov after improvement checks. Names are Live 12's: the LFO device's Rate, Time Mode and Waveform (Max for Live modulators), Sampler's auxiliary envelope. Nothing unused moved by a bit: a reference song using everything the engine had before (six instruments, chance and conditions, swing, a route, a sample, the fill key, a punch press) hashes 0x61e92430de04fa4c on a004aee and after, and again with every new command sent in its off form.

- **1. Modulation sources on drums, fx and the sampler:** done. Each has a layer lfo (rate 0.05 to 20 Hz, or synced with the time mode toggle to 1/32 to 4 bars, 1 bar by default; waveforms sine, up, down, triangle, square, random as sample and hold), and the sampler its aux env (attack 1 ms to 2 s, decay 10 ms to 4 s). The same chips and rings as the synths: drag the lfo (or the aux env) onto any knob; tap the chip and its own settings open above the knobs (`SourcePanel` in `src/components/m1/Modulation.tsx`) while every knob shows its amount. The settings are new knobs in a new bank, `mod`, appended to the drum rack, fx and sampler tables, so no saved knob moves. A synced lfo follows the song's beat, so it is the same every play. Measured: square synced to 1/2 onto the drums' pan by 0.25 flips -7.65 / +7.65 dB a beat, against -7.66 / +7.66 for the pan knob at 0.75 and 0.25; every waveform read back within 0.001 of its shape at the song's beat (1480 reads each); 2 Hz free running gives 8 rising zero crossings in 4 s; random holds one value a cycle and is the same with the same seed (song hash equal) and different with another; the fx noise hit's level range is 28.8 dB left alone, 59.8 dB with its lfo on the part's cutoff; the sampler's aux env onto pan by 0.25 reads -4.59 dB at 25 ms (pan 0.75 reads -5.87) and back to centre (0.00 dB) at 0.8 s.
- **2. Rings that move while the sound plays:** done. While a modulated knob is on screen the engine keeps that part's live values (new `cmd::MOD_WATCH`, `ccd_mod_values`: the lfo, the envelope, then each slot's knob as it plays) and the worklet sends them 30 times a second, 15 on a phone with four cores or fewer, nothing when no screen asks. Each ring gets a dot where the source has the knob this moment, painted on the DOM on the next animation frame (`useModLive`), so React never re-renders for it. The voice's own routes (the lfo and filter env on the cutoff) move too: watched, the engine runs the envelope source even with nothing routed (peaks at 0.991, holds at its 0.400 sustain). A slot's reported value is exactly what the engine plays (0.5 + 0.3 x lfo to 1e-6); watching never changes the sound (hash equal watched and not).
- **3. Chance and trig conditions in the midi export:** done, as heard by default. A .mid holds notes and velocities and nothing for chance (Live keeps chance per note in its own Live Set; we found no Ableton page saying a .mid carries it, nor any "chance as velocity" convention, so we did not build that), so "to live" on a song with chance, conditions or a free-running part asks: as you hear it (at least 8 bars and 4 times round, or the arrangement, with the engine's own seeded roll on the notes as the engine gets them, swung) or every note (chance left out, the file as before). Measured: eight passes of a pattern with 50% steps, 1:2 and 3:4 at seed 7, the engine plays `0 1 2 3 4 | 0 1 4 5 | 0 1 2 3 4 6 | 0 3 4 7 | 0 2 3 4 7 | 0 3 4 5 | 0 1 2 3 4 6 | 0 4 5` (and seq::plays agrees), and the exported file holds exactly those notes. A song with none of these writes the same bytes as before. In Live this matches recording the clip's output.
- **4. Free-running part lengths:** done, an option next to the restart (Elektron's master length at inf): the part row's restart key turns to free. In an unarranged song the engine runs the part at its own length and position (new `cmd::PART_LEN`), counting its own plays for chance and conditions; in an arrangement the part is laid across the whole song from its first beat. Measured on a 3-step part against a one-bar loop over three bars: restarting, beats 4 and 8 are the kick (48 hits); free, beat 4 is the snare and beat 8 the hat (40 hits, its 1:2 hat on the part's own even plays), the kick every 0.75 beats all the way. Default stays restart; old songs play as before. The export as heard keeps a free part drifting across all eight bars.
- **5. Punch-ins recorded into the song:** done. With the studio's rec key on, each press, slide and let-go of a punch-in key comes back from the engine with the beat you heard (new `out::PUNCHED`, the touch time carried like a note's) and becomes a punch in the song (`punches`, optional, cleaned on load; a new take replaces one it overlaps). The engine plays them back on their sample (new `cmd::PUNCH_CLEAR`, `PUNCH_ADD`, `PUNCH_COMMIT`), timed to the mix it acts on; a hand on a key wins over the automation. The mix mode shows them as a lane on the song's loop under the keys: tap one to change its amount or delete it, or clear them all. Measured: a filter automated at beat 1 changes the output first at sample 24,022 (beat 1 is heard at 24,023) and lets go exactly 381 samples (the 8 ms fade) after beat 2 is heard; held, -10.9 dB against dry; a beat repeat held by hand across an automated tape stop is bit for bit the hand alone; presses report beat 0.9990 when heard at 0.9990.
- **6. Sample-exact p-locks:** done. Locks ride on their notes to the engine (new `cmd::EV_LOCK`, up to 8 per note, `withEngineLocks` in `src/lib/plock.ts`): each note carries its locked value or "back to the knob", and the engine sets it on the note's own sample before it sounds (strip params jump, so the whole hit gets it); the screen timer that sent them before is gone. Measured: a kick's tune locked +7 st on beat 2 changes the output first at sample 48,018, the kick's first sound is 48,017; that kick is within -122 dB of the same kick played with the knob at +7 st (the first kick's tail, that far down, carries into a retrigger), and so is the next kick against its own; the same change sent 5 ms late, as the old timer could, is off by +5.4 dB of the kick's peak. A hat's pan locked hard left changes the output first on that step's first sound (sample 30,017 both) and leaves its right channel -149.5 dB down; the next step is back at centre (0.00 dB).
- **CPU** (`engine/tests/bench.rs`, the full eight-layer song, run alternately three times on the shared machine, best of five inside each): before 22.9 / 22.3 / 29.1%, after with nothing new in use 23.0 / 22.6 / 24.4%, after with everything in use (`CCD_BENCH_LEFTOVERS=1`: both drum parts with a random lfo on pan and cutoff and every step p-locked twice, two parts free running, the filter punched in and out every beat by automation, a part watched) 22.4 / 23.4 / 23.4%. No change we can measure.

## The short version

Expensive software instruments are not magic. Reviewers and the people who build them point at the same handful of things, and each one is a known method we can build, measure and play to you side by side. We do the twelve that people hear most, in the order that gives the most sound for the work, and we prove each one twice: a measured test in the engine, and an a/b clip on a "hear the difference" page where anyone can switch between the old engine and the new one on the same phrase.

The before clips are already rendered from today's engine (`public/audio/ab/before`, twelve phrases: kick, snare, clap, hats, kit, bass, lead, pad, reverb, echo, drive, the whole song). Every change gets rendered into an after set, so the page is honest by construction.

## Where the engine is today

Good bones, and honest about the gaps.

- **Already right:** sample-accurate timing at any buffer size; 2x oversampling with a clean half-band decimator; zero-delay-feedback ladder and state-variable filters (the same filter maths u-he and Ableton use); band-limited oscillators; an 8-line reverb with Hadamard mixing; a glue compressor and true-peak limiter; measured tests for every knob we show. As of today: an lfo, a 2-operator fm voice and a separate filter envelope on the lead and chords, each with a test that proves it changes the sound.
- **What a trained ear hears now:** drive and crush fizz when pushed (aliasing, the classic "cheap digital" sound); every voice is identical to the last, so chords and pads sound static; the hats are a filtered mix of square waves and noise, which reads as generated; the kick is one pitch sweep with no attack shape; unison detune is a straight line rather than the curve that makes supersaws open up; there is one filter character; the reverb has no modulation, so long tails ring.

## Why some synths sound expensive

From the research, five things, each with sources:

1. **Filters solved properly, with the distortion inside them.** Zero-delay feedback keeps resonance correct when the filter moves fast; saturation in each stage gives it character. u-he says this is why Diva's resonance sounds "more authentic"; reviewers A/B'd Diva against a restored Minimoog and it "held its own".
2. **No aliasing around anything that distorts.** Anti-derivative anti-aliasing (ADAA) plus 2x oversampling makes even a hard clipper "practically a non-issue". This is the single biggest cheap-versus-expensive tell.
3. **Every voice slightly different.** Ableton built Drift around this: each voice gets its own small random detune of oscillators and filter, seeded per instance so a saved sound recalls exactly. Diva calls the same thing trimmers. A reviewer calling an emulation's oscillators "too stable" is describing its absence.
4. **Drive and the master chain.** Roland's TR-8S got "the master distortion and drive lack warmth"; the Digitakt II "just sounds immense", credited to its drive and compressor. The last stage decides whether a box sounds digital or big.
5. **Drums from the circuit, not from noise.** The published analysis of the 808 shows its hats have no noise generator at all: six square waves at measured, inharmonic frequencies, band-passed near 3.4 kHz and 7.1 kHz, with a resonant peak near 10.5 kHz. The kick has a 6 ms pitch bump that makes the attack punchy and a slow pitch "sigh" over the tail.

## The twelve, in build order

Each line: what it is, what you will hear, how we prove it, rough size.

1. **ADAA on every drive, crush and clip stage.** You will hear drive add warmth and harmonics instead of fizz, most on bass and kick. Proof: an aliasing test (energy above the harmonics of a 3 kHz tone through full drive, before and after), and the drive clip. Days.
2. **Every voice its own voice card.** Small, slow, per-voice differences in pitch, filter and envelope times, seeded per song so it recalls exactly. You will hear chords beat gently and pads breathe. Proof: a test that two voices on the same note differ by a measured amount and the same seed gives identical audio; the pad clip. Days.
3. **808-measured hats and cymbals.** The six measured square-wave partials, the two band-passes, the resonant top. You will hear metal, not hiss. Proof: spectrum peaks at the band-pass centres; the hats clip. Days to a week.
4. **The supersaw detune curve.** The JP-8000's measured detune law, centre and side mix laws, random start phases. You will hear unison open up wide with an obvious sweet spot. Proof: the measured offsets in a test; the lead clip. Days.
5. **A kick built from the 808 circuit.** Shaped click, the 6 ms attack bump, the slow sigh, decay that rings out rather than fades, a hold stage, three body shapes. You will hear a real attack and a pitch that breathes. Proof: pitch track over time against the paper's shape; the kick clip. One to two weeks.
6. **The master chain done properly.** An SSL-style glue (ratios 2, 4, 10, auto release, a low cut on what it listens to, a mix knob), a true-peak limiter that checks between samples at -1 dBTP, a loudness meter, and exports at -14 LUFS so a learner's song plays on Spotify exactly as it did in the lesson. You will hear the song glue and get loud without pumping. Proof: true-peak and LUFS tests against the broadcast standard; the song clip. One to two weeks.
7. **Three filter characters.** Keep the ladder (warm, loses bass as resonance rises), add an MS-20 style (screams when pushed) and an Oberheim-style 2-pole (smooth, sweet). Drive before the filter, asymmetric, with a second stage near +6 dB like Drift. You will hear three genuinely different personalities on the same bass line. Proof: frequency response per type, resonance stability under a fast envelope; a three-way bass clip. Two to three weeks.
8. **Drum voices with their own noise layer.** Tone and noise each get their own filter and envelope, and pitch can sweep, wobble or randomise per hit. You will hear snares with separate snap and body, and metallic percussion. Proof: tests per layer; the snare clip. One to two weeks.
9. **A modulated plate and a smoother hall.** A Dattorro plate and the current hall with slow modulation and darker, frequency-dependent decay. You will hear smooth tails that don't ring or muddy the kick. Proof: decay time per band, modal density; the reverb clip. Two to three weeks.
10. **Plucks, mallets and keys from a resonator.** A tuned comb and two-resonator mode (the idea behind Ableton's Collision and Elektron's comb filters). You will hear plucked strings, marimbas and a surprisingly convincing piano from a click. Proof: pitch accuracy across the keyboard; a pluck clip. Two weeks.
11. **A tape and analogue delay.** Wow and flutter that depend on delay time, repeats that darken, the ducking of old bucket-brigade units. You will hear dub delays that feel like hardware. Proof: modulation depth versus delay time; the echo clip. One to two weeks.
12. **A clean wavetable oscillator, fm feedback and phase distortion.** One band-limited table per octave so high notes stay clean; fm feedback that goes from saw to noise musically; the Casio CZ trick of filter sweeps with no filter. Proof: aliasing tests up the keyboard; a lead clip. Two weeks.

**Enablers you won't hear but need:** four voices per SIMD lane (Ableton rewrote Drift's filter this way) so phones have room for all of the above; staying at 2x oversampling and keeping 4x for the limiter only; a compute ceiling of about 1 ms per audio block.

**Future-ready, after the twelve:** per-note pressure and slide from multi-touch (MPE), custom tunings, a "pro" desktop mode with neural saturation. On-device stem separation (pull the kick out of a reference track and compare it to yours) is a strong lesson idea, but it needs a graphics card and runs offline, so it is a later feature.

**Deliberately not doing:** spectral resynthesis (Serum 2's headline, large, not something a learner hears first), and claiming "component-level circuit simulation". We don't do that, and the research shows how fast that claim gets tested.

## The instruments and sampler: a world apart

The research's twelve interface moves, applied to every instrument screen. In build order:

1. **Touch to reveal.** Any knob or pad you touch shows its name and value on the screen, beside your finger, never under it.
2. **Colour binds control to picture.** Each knob's colour is the colour of the line it moves on the screen.
3. **Dark until used.** Whatever isn't doing anything is dim; turning it up lights it.
4. **Three honest moving pictures, everywhere:** the envelope shape with a dot running along it on every note, the filter curve whose knee moves with cutoff, the lfo dot travelling its wave. Driven by the engine's real state, not a guess.
5. **Instant slices.** A sample lands already sliced, one slider re-slices live, every slice on a numbered pad that flashes as it plays, one-tap undo.
6. **Hold a step, turn a knob:** that step alone takes the value, and turns a second colour (Elektron's parameter locks, Move's step automation).
7. **Capture instead of record.** The app always listens; play first, keep it after. Ghost notes until you keep them.
8. **Pads that feel like pads.** Velocity from where you tap, 48 px minimum, a ring flash on every hit.
9. **Phone-safe note editing.** Tap to add, hold to select, an edge handle for length (drag it up and down for velocity), pinch for the grid, and a strip of buttons for nudge, transpose and quantise.
10. **DJ waveforms coloured by frequency** with bar numbers, phrase labels and a green or amber key badge; cutting the low eq takes the red out of the wave.
11. **Fill and randomise with an amount,** for drums, arpeggios and the note grid.
12. **The lesson lights one control.** Everything else dims, every hit gets a green, amber or red verdict, and practice loops speed up as you get it.

**Built (5 Nov, instrument pass):** envelope and filter pictures from the engine's own maths (`src/lib/shapes.ts`, `Pictures.tsx`), with engine item 7's three filter characters; knob rings in their picture's colour, dark until used (`src/lib/paramColor.ts`); pads with velocity from tap height and a hit ring; hold a step, turn a knob (`src/lib/plock.ts`, played from the host at each step, 80 ms look-ahead); capture (`src/lib/capture.ts`); phone note editing in the piano roll; fill and randomise for drum, bass and lead steps, the note grid and the arp (`src/lib/dice.ts`); dj waveforms coloured by frequency (`src/lib/djWaves.ts`, bank measured offline by `scripts/dj-waves.ts`); one lit control per shape step, verdicts with ms on every play-in hit, "faster" after three clean takes.
Still open: per-event locks inside the engine (the host sends them now, so a lock lands within a few ms, not sample-exact); touch-to-reveal beside the finger (values show on the screen and under the knob); instant slices as their own pass (slices already on lit pads); pinch for grid resolution in the piano roll; the arp screen showing the varied notes it will play; a scrolling zoomed deck view beside the 16-bar overview.

## What we can say in marketing (and what we can't)

Supported by the research and provable by our own tests:
- "Filters solved the way u-he and Ableton solve them: zero-delay feedback, with the distortion inside the filter."
- "Drums built from the measured 808 circuit, not filtered noise."
- "Anti-aliased saturation everywhere, so drive adds harmonics, not hash."
- "Every voice is a slightly different voice card, like a real polysynth."
- "A loudness-aware master: your song sounds the same on Spotify as it did in the lesson."
- "Hear the difference": a public a/b page with our old engine and new engine on the same phrases, level-matched so loudness never decides it.

Not supported, so we don't say it: "component-level circuit simulation", "sounds identical to an 808", "better than Ableton". If Param's ears say a sound beats a reference in a blind test, we can say what was tested and how.

## How we'll know it worked

- Every item has an engine test with a number in it, run on every build.
- Every item adds an after clip; the a/b page plays before and after on the same phrase, level-matched.
- You do a blind a/b on each before it ships (the rule in `school-process.md`: never claim a sound matches Ableton without an ear test).
- Performance: the full eight-layer song stays under the compute ceiling on a mid-range Android phone and an older iPhone.

## Decisions for Param

1. Order: start with the four cheapest and most audible (ADAA, voice cards, 808 hats, supersaw), then the kick and the master chain. Or reorder.
2. The a/b page: public from day one (marketing), or private until the first four land.
3. Filter characters: three names a learner sees. Proposal: "warm" (ladder), "scream" (MS-20 style), "smooth" (Oberheim style).
4. Export loudness: -14 LUFS by default, with a "loud" option for club play.
